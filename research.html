Research

Research Interests

My research combines behavioral economics, experimental economics, and neuroeconomics with perspectives from psychology, philosophy, and religion to better understand human choice behavior under risk and uncertainty.

Publications

Distribution-Dependent Number Preferences

This paper examines how individuals select numbers in lottery games, finding that 40–50% of subjects exhibit distribution-dependent preferences and an illusion of control, which helps explain observed patterns in real-world lotteries.
